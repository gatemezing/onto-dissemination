ERA Route Book Elements

The elements an infrastructure manager owes a railway undertaking for the Route Book, as listed in Appendix D2 of the TSI OPE (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/773). Pick a country and one of its national lines: the tool collects every property carrying era:tsiOPEAppendixD2Index along that line and at its operational points, in kilometre order, and reports which of the 46 D2 elements the line actually covers. Export to CSV or Excel. Queries graph.data.era.europa.eu live from your browser.

Appendix D2 numbers the route book; the ontology carries the numbers but not the headings.
      ERA annotates each property with era:tsiOPEAppendixD2Index — the index of that term in
      “Appendix D2: Elements the infrastructure manager has to provide to the railway undertaking for the Route
      Book” — but publishes no title for each index. Elements are therefore named here by their ontology labels,
      with the D2 number kept as the legal cross-reference. One D2 element is often several properties, and one
      property often serves several D2 elements.

    
Five D2 elements cannot be filled by anyone today. 3.2.4, 3.2.6,
      3.3.5 and 3.3.6 are carried only by era:SpecialArea, and
      3.4.7 only by era:RadioBlockCenter; neither class has a single instance in the live
      endpoint. The coverage view separates that case — not published by anyone — from an element that other
      managers do publish but this line does not, because they mean very different things to a route-book compiler.

    
D2 1.1 takes two registers, and the same URI opens both. Every section of line declares its
      manager: era:infrastructureManager points at an era:OrganisationRole, which
      era:roleOf resolves to the organisation. RINF knows which organisation but not its name —
      all 660 era:Body resources in rinf-plus carry zero foaf:name. The name
      comes from the Organisation
      Codes Register, queried with the very same URIs RINF returned — no code column, no join key, no matching by
      hand. That is what a shared identifier buys. The dataset a line is published in is reported alongside, as the
      separate fact it is: German line 4000 appears in two of them.

    
Managers model their networks differently, and the tool absorbs the differences
      rather than dropping the countries that have them. Every infrastructure manager publishes national
      lines; not all publish them the same way, and a query written against one style silently returns nothing for
      the others. Four variations are handled here:

    
    
      
  • Line identity. 9,543 of the 9,642 era:LinearPositioningSystem resources carry
        era:lineId. The 99 that do not are Croatia's 55 and Norway's 32, which put the identifier in the
        English rdfs:label instead — NationalRailwayLine_M604,
        B03-Kongsvingerbanen. The line list takes era:lineId where it exists and the label
        where it does not, and says which it used.
    • 
      
  • Reaching operational points. Most managers position a point with a kilometric post on the
        line's own LRS. Croatia's 582 points carry era:uopid and a net reference but nothing tying them
        to an LRS, and no era:inCountry either — yet its sections name them with
        era:opStart and era:opEnd. Both routes are queried, and the second excludes points
        the first already found. It also improves complete networks: French line 830000-1 goes from 327
        points to 603.
    • 
      
  • Linear referencing at all. Croatia's net point references carry a geometry and no
        era:hasLrsCoordinate, so its sections have no kilometre position. Position is therefore
        optional: unpositioned places are reported with — and sorted after the positioned ones, rather
        than excluded. Setting a kilometre range necessarily limits results to places that have one.
    • 
      
  • Organisation URIs. Names are joined to the Organisation Codes Register on
        era:organisationCode, not on the body URI. The URI join works for 25 of the 27 — but Croatia
        publishes its manager under both body/organisation/0078 and a bare …/0078_ORG, and
        Norway uses …/organisations/0076 where the register has body/organisation/0076.
    • 
    

    
None of this is guesswork about what a manager meant: each branch queries a property the ontology defines, and
      the page reports which branch produced what. The queries are
      published alongside the page.

    
Superseded descriptions are dropped, place by place. The same validity rule as the RCC tool:
      the newest window per stretch of line, and per era:uopid then per track at operational points.
      Ten countries publish no dated validity at all, and a resource with no validity is always kept.
      Retired (owl:deprecated) properties are excluded.
