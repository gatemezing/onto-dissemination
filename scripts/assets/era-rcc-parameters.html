ERA RCC Parameters

Route-compatibility parameters for one national line, positioned by kilometre post. Pick a country, then a line published by that country, and the tool returns the RINF parameters flagged era:usedInRCCCalculations — those along the line (index 1.1) and those at its operational points (index 1.2). Only the latest validity window is used, and retired parameters are excluded. Export to CSV or Excel. Queries graph.data.era.europa.eu live from your browser.

Superseded descriptions are dropped, and the comparison is made place by place — never line by
      line. Some managers republish the same stretch every year under a new validity window: German line
      4000 returns 16,615 rows unfiltered and 8,281 once each stretch keeps only its own latest window.
      Others date a section by the day it opened: French line 830000-1 spreads 25 different beginnings
      across its 570 sections, so a line-wide "newest date" would keep two sections and delete the rest. The newest
      window is therefore picked per stretch of line, and at operational points per era:uopid and then
      per track within the point — France repeats tracks inside a point, Germany repeats the whole station
      as separate resources sharing one uopid, and both rules run.

    
Countries that publish no dates are untouched. Ten of the countries offered here — Sweden,
      Spain, Ireland, Estonia, Greece, Finland, Romania, Croatia, Austria and Luxembourg — carry no dated validity
      at all, and Liechtenstein publishes no sections of line to date. Filtering on the newest date would delete
      every one of their rows, so a resource with no validity is always kept.

    
Retired parameters are excluded. 4 of the 87 properties flagged
      era:usedInRCCCalculations are owl:deprecated and are left out, as in the other tools
      here — three of them are replaced by a live property under the same RINF number, so only
      1.1.1.2.2.1.2.1 disappears entirely. Values that are concepts are resolved to their English label
      where one exists, and a track carrying several values for one parameter has them joined with
      “ | ”.
