01Summary

The CRD graph replicates RNE's Common Reference Data every day: TAF/TAP primary locations (PLC) and subsidiary locations (SLC). It uses only ERA v3.3.4 terms, and every term it uses exists, undeprecated, in the PROD ontology. Its PLC structure is cleaner than RINF's. Each code has one IRI, and every location is named in English, flagged, dated and attributed to an IM, with a valid code pattern throughout. Against ERA's own shapes, CRD still conforms better than RINF: under PROD only the NUTS vocabulary check fails, plus a geometry Warning for the PLs that have no coordinates.

What the comparison with RINF shows

  • Coverage. 44,443 PLCs are in both sources. 14,814 are in CRD only (GB 7,312, DE 2,036, FR 962, PT 825, PL 685, RS 682, …); 4,381 are in RINF only (DE 1,935, CH 922, IT 307, PL 271, NO 269, DK 216, EU 91, …).
  • 4,356 PLCs that RINF operational points reference are absent from CRD. Most belong to DB InfraGO (1,934) and the Swiss graphs 0085 + 3915 (905).
  • RINF is partly CRD-derived. ProRail's 780 PLs carry this pipeline's IRI fingerprints, so agreement there is reported separately. Italy's RFI and Luxembourg's CFL now agree with CRD on every field without them, so they are probably loaded from CRD as well.
  • Validity start dates disagree on 47 % of the independent overlap. The most likely reason is that the two sources date different events.
  • Subsidiary locations do not overlap. RINF's 3,899 SLs are Bane NOR tracks; CRD's 76,786 are the RNE SLC catalogue. Not one (PLC, SLC) pair is shared.

03Conformance with the ERA ontology and shapes

3.1 Ontology

  • Vocabulary. All the classes and properties the data uses are declared in ERA PROD v3.3.4, none deprecated, with the expected domains and ranges. The deprecated era:tafTAPCode is not used.
  • CRD's copy of the ontology is graph-isomorphic to PROD's once line endings are normalised. 296 literals use \r\n where PROD uses \n.
  • ParameterApplicability is modelled correctly. It is a subclass of era:TemporalFeature, so time:hasBeginning directly on it is right. None of the 54,532 applicabilities contradicts its flag.
  • Used terms (F6, resolved). On 3 November both data graphs carried full ERA descriptions of 37 terms, including owl:Ontology <http://data.europa.eu/949/>. They now hold data only, and a separate graph/era/used-terms (46 triples) lists the 37 terms with their rdf:type and nothing else.

Sampling artifacts. sh:class results on values at the last hop of the sample are false positives, because the sample does not copy their rdf:type. The validation script drops them (409 under PROD and 410 under UAT on RINF, for kmPost, hasTopoCoordinate, body role and inNUTS3). Checked live across rinf-plus on 20 November, every such value carries its expected type. The first version of this review, before 6 November, still listed some of them.

Bane NOR does model km posts differently, but the shapes do not check it. All 732 posts hang on one nationwide LPS (_lps02, no era:lineId) as an integer grid from km −2 to 729, and none of its 35 line LPSs carries a post. Its 24,045 coordinates reference the nearest post, with offsets from −500 to +500 m. Every other large publisher references the preceding post on a line LPS.

3.4 Notes for ERA's shape maintainers

These problems in ERA's own shapes came to light while running them. They have nothing to do with CRD.

  • PROD LangStringEnRequired and LangStringEnRequiredIfPresent are invalid SPARQL once $PATH is substituted, because SELECT $this $PATH projects an IRI. UAT already reads SELECT $this.
  • The ontology declares both geo: and geosparql: for one namespace. rdflib-based validators bind only one, so the other stops resolving.
  • Constraints use VALUES, MINUS and nested SELECT, which SHACL 1.0's pre-binding rules forbid; pyshacl rejects them by default.
  • 17 UAT constraints use SERVICE and cannot run on a local copy.
  • subsidiaryLocationType is optional in PROD and UAT. A publisher can drop every type, as CRD did for 20,020 SLs (F3), without a single result. If the type is meant to be there, add sh:minCount 1.
  • UAT relaxed HasTopoCoordinate between 3 and 20 November: it no longer requires a topological coordinate per NetPointReference. 175,794 of RINF's 324,968 have none, so about 1,300 results vanished from the RINF sample without any data change.
  • Severity on sh:SPARQLConstraint nodes is ignored. The intended Warnings report as Violations.
  • era:inNUTS3 points at a scheme with no members (F10).

05Findings in detail

F1RESOLVEDAllocation company roles

On 3 November every era:allocationCompany pointed at an …/organisationRole/{code}_IM role, so the graph said UIC, DB Cargo and nine other non-IMs were infrastructure managers (30,059 SLs). The role now follows the company: 23 companies are IM, 9 are RU-3 (freight railway undertaking: Rail Cargo Austria, DB Cargo, ČD Cargo, SBB Cargo, UIRR, …), Rail&Sea is Keeper and UIC is SB. Checked against OCR-KG (scripts/allocation_roles.py), 33 of the 34 hold that role there. The exception is UIC (18,435 SLs), which OCR-KG lists only as IRO, a code ERA's role vocabulary does not define. SB ("Sector Organisation") is in it, so CRD's choice is the one ERA's vocabulary supports (F12).

F2RESOLVEDGreat Britain at null island

On 3 November all 7,312 GB PLs, plus one each in DE, FR and CZ, had geo:asWKT "POINT(0 0)". They now have no geometry at all, which UAT reports as its intended geometry warning instead of drawing them in the Gulf of Guinea.

F3MAJORSubsidiary-location types from UAT changed on 20 November

On 3 November 19,990 SLs used type codes 01, 15, 03, 10, 27 and 02, which exist in the UAT scheme (50 codes) but not in PROD's (37). CRD now loads PROD's SKOS as it is, and drops the type for those SLs instead: every one of the 19,965 still present lost its era:subsidiaryLocationType, and the 55 new SLs with those codes arrived untyped. 20,020 SLs (26.1 %) now have no type: CZ 12,926, SK 5,155, HR 1,633, others 306. The RNE code still shows in the IRI (…/subsidiaryLocation/{PLC}_{SLC}_01_{company}), so the information exists but is no longer data.

Assert the type and load the UAT SubsidiaryLocationTypes scheme (or the six concepts) with it, or validate against UAT throughout, and record the choice in the dataset metadata. Dropping data to pass a shape is the wrong trade.

F4RESOLVEDSL properties outside the UAT closed shape

UAT closes SubsidiaryLocationShape. On 3 November every SL carried era:inCountry, era:infrastructureManager and era:operatingLanguage, which it does not allow. None does now; the country, the IM and the language can be read from the linked PL, and this review now does so.

F5MAJORDanish codes identify different places RINF side · verified on samples · unchanged

For 294 of the 302 shared DK codes, the CRD and RINF positions are more than 10 km apart. For 236 of them, CRD's position of one code lies within 100 m of RINF's position of another Danish code (235 on 3 November, counted the same way). For example, RINF's OP uopid DK00010 "Høje Taastrup" sits at POINT(12.269823 55.648536), which is CRD's DK00005 Høje Taastrup; CRD's DK00010 is Borup, 25 km away. RINF's Danish PLs carry no names, so name matching cannot catch the error.

Report this to Banedanmark and ERA. Until it is fixed, do not join Danish data on code.

F6–F9MINORModelling and hygiene

F6, resolved. The data graphs no longer re-declare ontology terms. F7, unchanged. era:NetBasicReference carries untyped wgs84:lat/lon next to a typed geo:wktLiteral (51,942 references); drop the WGS84 triples. F8, unchanged. None appears in 2,269 temporal-feature and 51,371 applicability IRIs, apparently a serialised Python None. The same IRIs exist in RINF's ProRail graph, so rename them only together with ERA. F9, resolved. No name contains a run of whitespace any more (613 on 3 November).

F10MINORNUTS IRIs partly on the ERA side

The ontology declares era:inNUTS3's scheme as https://publications.europa.eu/resource/authority/nuts, but no concept in ERA-Onto is skos:inScheme it. ERA's graph/nuts uses http://data.europa.eu/nuts/code/{code}, which CRD follows (9,456 PLs). RINF now uses both forms: Greece's OSE (212 PLs) the same as CRD, and the Austrian publishers (88 PLs) the Publications Office IRIs. A join on IRI between CRD and Austria returns nothing, and InNUTS3SKOS fires for every value in either form.

ERA should settle on one IRI form, and CRD and RINF should follow it.

F11SUGGESTIONGovernance and provenance partly resolved

IRIs. All CRD resources are minted under ERA's namespace by a third party. For PLs this is useful, because 36,676 codes share the IRI with RINF, but it also means CRD triples merge silently with ERA's. Agree the pattern with ERA, or mint under a CRD namespace and link with owl:sameAs. Provenance, mostly done. The metadata now names the publisher and the CRD snapshot with its generation time; add a licence. SKOS, done. The vocabulary graph is now PROD's (70,705 triples), which is what turned F3 into dropped types. Validity. CRD appears to drop retired codes. Keeping them with a closed validity window would keep RINF's references to them resolvable.

F12SUGGESTIONUIC's role differs between CRD and OCR-KG new

CRD types UIC's allocation role SB ("Sector Organisation"); OCR-KG gives UIC the role IRO, which ERA's organisation-roles vocabulary does not define. 18,435 SLs are affected.

Either OCR-KG uses a defined concept or ERA adds IRO; CRD then follows OCR-KG.

06Method, reproducibility and limits

The queries, scripts and output files are all in crd-kg/. Each field is extracted by its own query, which keeps RINF under its 120 s limit, and the extracts are joined locally on era:primaryLocationCode.

python3 -I scripts/fetch.py                # ~1 min, both endpoints → data/raw/ (git-ignored)
python3 -I scripts/compare.py 2026-11-20   # joins on the PLC → stats/*.csv, stats/summary.json
python3 -I scripts/allocation_roles.py     # F1: CRD allocation roles against OCR-KG
python3 -I scripts/sparql.py crd queries/crd-checks.rq
python  -I scripts/validate.py SAMPLE.ttl SHAPES.ttl OUT.csv REF1.ttl REF2.ttl …

Limits.

  • Join key. Where a code means different things on each side (F5), agreement figures say nothing for that country.
  • RINF duplicates. RINF was not deduplicated for yearly republishing, and a field counts as agreeing when it matches any RINF variant. This flatters RINF slightly.
  • SHACL scope. SHACL ran on a sample; every constraint that fired was then counted exactly over the full CRD dataset. The 17 SERVICE constraints were skipped, and pyshacl's pre-binding checks were relaxed so that ERA's queries run as ERA runs them.
  • Independence. Only IRI fingerprints mark a RINF graph as CRD-derived. IT and LU agree on every field without them, so the independent figures may still include copies of CRD.
  • Roles. F1 and F12 rely on OCR-KG's role list as of 20 November 2026.
